## FAERS & VigiAccess Signal Analysis — Sitagliptin

## Overview
An independent pharmacovigilance project analyzing real-world adverse event reporting data for **Sitagliptin** (brand: Bryonium), a DPP-4 inhibitor used in Type 2 Diabetes, using two public pharmacovigilance databases: the **FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS)** and **WHO VigiAccess**. This project is part of a broader 3-drug methodology exercise (Metformin, Sitagliptin, Amoxicillin) demonstrating a repeatable approach to signal detection and reporting pattern analysis, rather than a cross-database comparison.

## Objective
To practice real-world pharmacovigilance data extraction, categorization, and interpretation using publicly available spontaneous reporting databases — reaction frequency, reporter demographics, and reporting patterns.

## Data Sources
- FAERS Public Dashboard: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/questions-and-answers-fdas-adverse-event-reporting-system-faers/fda-adverse-event-reporting-system-faers-public-dashboard
- WHO VigiAccess: https://www.vigiaccess.org/

## Interpretation
The vast majority of FAERS reports (92.6%) came from healthcare professionals, suggesting good quality/reliability of case data. Hypoglycemia was the leading individual reaction reported in FAERS, consistent with Sitagliptin's known mechanism of action (potentiating insulin secretion). VigiAccess data showed gastrointestinal disorders as the largest reaction category by volume, reflecting the broader global reporting pool.

## Limitations
- Spontaneous reporting databases are subject to underreporting and reporting bias
- No causality assessment was performed (this is a descriptive signal-detection exercise)
- Data reflects raw case counts, not adjusted disproportionality measures (PRR/ROR)
